\section*{Optimal Perks at the Hydrothermal Fabrication Laboratory}

The graduate students in the Kahlert School of Computing at the University of
Utah basically live in the lab---and in the Hydrothermal Fabrication Laboratory
(the department's very serious name for the grad student laundromat).

To keep morale high (and clothing less hazardous), the department offers special
laundry perks: things like hypoallergenic detergent, extra-softener cycles,
express drying, anti-static treatments, and so on. Each type of perk:
\begin{itemize}
    \item has a ``freshness rating'' that measures how much the grad students
    love/benefit from it, and
    \item costs a certain number of dollars from the department's laundry
    subsidy budget.
\end{itemize}

Every year, the department runs a survey to estimate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item how much the students value each perk (its freshness rating), and
    \item how much each perk would cost to offer for the semester.
\end{itemize}

Unfortunately, the budget is limited.

\medskip

\noindent
Given a total budget of \(N\) dollars and the freshness ratings and costs for
all the laundry perks, determine which combination of perks the department
should offer to maximize the total freshness for the grad students.

You may choose at most one of each perk (you cannot choose fractional perks, and
you can't ``half-offer'' a perk).
